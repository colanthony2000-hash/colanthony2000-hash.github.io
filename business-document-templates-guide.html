Business guide

Business documents should be clear, reusable, and easy to approve.

Good templates save time, but the best documents still reflect the exact client, role, policy, offer, or transaction in front of you.

Core documents

Build a practical document library.

Most SMEs and professional teams need reusable formats for invoices, quotations, proposals, business letters, employment letters, HR policies, meeting minutes, agreements, and internal memos.

A template should include the correct structure, but it should not sound generic. Replace placeholders with specific facts, responsibilities, deadlines, prices, names, and approval details.

For sensitive legal, tax, HR, or compliance documents, get professional review before use. Templates are productivity support, not a substitute for qualified advice.

Quality control

Review the document before it represents your business.

Start with purpose and audience. A proposal written for a new client should not read like an internal memo, and an employment letter should not leave essential terms to assumption. Check that the opening explains why the document exists and that the final section tells the reader what happens next.

Verify every factual field, including legal names, registration details, dates, prices, taxes, account information, deadlines, signatures, and referenced attachments. Remove instructions and placeholder text before export.

Use a consistent heading system, readable type, sensible spacing, and a filename that identifies the client or transaction. Store an approved final copy separately from the editable template so later users do not overwrite the source.

AccuracyNames, figures, dates, claims, and attachments match the underlying records.
CompletenessThe reader can understand the offer, responsibility, deadline, and next action.
AuthorityThe correct person reviews and signs the document before it is issued.
Version controlDrafts, approvals, and final copies are labelled and stored clearly.

Common questions

Business document FAQ

When is a template enough?

A template is useful for routine structure. Transactions involving legal rights, regulation, tax, employment disputes, major funding, or unusual risk should receive qualified professional review.

Should one proposal template be used for every client?

Keep a reusable structure, but rewrite the problem, scope, evidence, deliverables, timeline, pricing, and next step for the specific client.

Can AI-generated wording be issued immediately?

No. Review it for factual errors, invented claims, unsuitable tone, confidentiality, and obligations the organisation did not intend to accept.

What should an invoice contain?

Include supplier and client details, a unique reference, issue and due dates, clear item descriptions, amounts, applicable tax, total, payment instructions, and relevant terms.
