Assessment guide

Continuous assessment should measure learning, not just collect marks.

Schools get better results when continuous assessment combines fair records, useful feedback, and tasks that match what was actually taught.

What to include

Use more than one evidence source.

A balanced continuous assessment system may include classwork, homework, tests, projects, practical work, oral responses, notebook checks, and participation. The right mix depends on the subject and class level.

Marks should be recorded consistently and explained clearly to learners. Where possible, assessment should help teachers identify gaps early, before terminal examination pressure begins.

Assessment questions should align with lesson objectives. If the class practised application, the assessment should not only test recall.

A repeatable assessment cycle

Plan, collect evidence, respond, and review.

Start by identifying the learning objective and the kind of evidence that can demonstrate it. A written quiz may suit factual recall, while a practical task, oral explanation, project, or demonstration may be more appropriate for applied skills.

Use a simple marking guide before learners begin. This improves consistency and makes expectations easier to explain. Record the date, task type, maximum mark, learner score, and any moderation or correction made later.

After marking, look beyond the class average. Check which questions produced common errors, which learners need additional support, and whether the teaching sequence should be adjusted. Continuous assessment is most valuable when it changes the next teaching decision.

Alignment checkCan the task actually show whether the stated lesson objective was achieved?
Evidence checkAre marks supported by work that can be reviewed, moderated, or explained?
Feedback checkDoes the learner know what was done well and what action should follow?
Record checkCan the school trace when, how, and by whom the score was awarded?

Common questions

Continuous assessment FAQ

Should every task carry the same weight?

No. Weight should reflect the importance, difficulty, and evidence quality of the task, while remaining consistent with the school's published assessment policy.

Can participation be scored?

It can be, but the criterion must be clear and inclusive. Avoid rewarding confidence or talkativeness when the intended skill is something different.

How often should records be reviewed?

Teachers should review records during the term, not only at report time. Department or school moderation should happen early enough to correct missing or inconsistent evidence.

Can AI prepare assessment questions?

AI can suggest questions and marking ideas, but a teacher must check curriculum alignment, accuracy, difficulty, bias, answer keys, and suitability for the learners.
