Operational fit

Public-sector technology needs governance before scale.

A tool should not bypass an authorised approval, records schedule, procurement rule, accessibility obligation, or security control. Begin by documenting the legal or policy basis for the process, responsible officers, required evidence, records classification, service standard, and escalation route. Pilot changes with real users and evaluate them before wider deployment.

Working method

A governed pilot pathway

The pilot should answer whether the solution is useful, lawful, secure, accessible, supportable, and affordable at the intended scale.

1. Establish authority and scope

Name the process owner, policy basis, participating unit, users, information classification, desired outcome, and exclusions.

2. Complete risk and readiness checks

Review procurement, security, privacy, records, accessibility, infrastructure, integration, support, and change-management needs.

3. Run a controlled pilot

Train users, preserve existing controls, record incidents and support requests, and collect agreed service and quality measures.

4. Document the decision

Compare outcomes, risks, total cost, user feedback, and remediation needs before expansion, redesign, or closure.

Rollout and safeguards

Test the workflow before expanding it.

Public information may include citizen, employee, financial, security, or legally protected records. Use approved systems and follow applicable Nigerian data-protection, records, procurement, and cybersecurity requirements. Do not enter classified, confidential, or identifiable case information into unapproved AI services. Generated policy or legal language must be reviewed by authorised professionals and should not be treated as an official instrument until properly approved.

Readiness checklist

  • The institution has assigned business, technical, information-security, privacy, records, and procurement owners.
  • Accessibility and infrastructure requirements reflect the actual users and locations.
  • The pilot preserves auditability and does not create an unofficial parallel record system.
  • Long-term support, training, licensing, exit, and data-portability needs are understood.

Common questions

What decision makers should clarify

Can JENECONK support a government pilot?

Yes, subject to an agreed scope, procurement process, institutional requirements, technical assessment, and delivery arrangement.

Can CBT be used for recruitment or promotion?

An institutional setup can be discussed. Security, identity, accessibility, question governance, support, scoring, appeals, and records requirements must be defined.

Is Smart Procurement ready for every agency?

Suitability depends on the agency's approval route, integrations, security, roles, reporting, procurement rules, deployment needs, and support model.

How should public-sector AI be governed?

Use approved purposes, minimum necessary data, human accountability, source verification, audit records, access controls, incident handling, and regular review.
